<CaseStudySection id="premise" title="What does the next operator inherit?" eyebrow="Premise">

At 19:00 one operator goes home and another takes over the building. What changed, what was left open on purpose, and what must they not touch?

The gap is not data. Systems keep what happened and lose why a person chose it.

> Put the right knowledge into the exact moment responsibility changes hands.

Neuron proposes, the operator decides, and the decision and its reason become the record the next shift inherits. For the arriving shift engineer, the costliest mistake is undoing a decision a colleague made on purpose.

<CaseStudySection id="chat" title="Chat is the right fallback and the wrong default" eyebrow="Position on chat">

A chat box asks for a question when the operator has the least time to form one, and its answer does not pass to the next shift. So every AI surface follows three rules:

- **It attaches to objects.** Selecting an asset is the query.
- **Its output is interface state.** Failure impact is drawn on the graph.
- **What you accept or override joins the record.** AI reasoning becomes inheritable.

Chat still suits the question nobody anticipated. I scoped it out.

</CaseStudySection>

<CaseStudySection id="decisions" title="The calls, and what each one argues against" eyebrow="Design decisions">

**Autonomy is shown, not implied.** Each recommendation lists what Neuron does alone, what needs approval, and what it must never touch. That promise builds more trust than a confidence score.

**Friction scales with reversibility.** Raising an alert threshold is one click. Transferring critical load takes four checks and the asset name typed back.

<ArtifactFrame marks={[{ x: 3.8, y: 55.7, w: 92.4, h: 32.4, label: "Friction scales with reversibility" }]} frame="none" src="/work/neuron-shift/gate.webp" alt="The decision dialog for transferring critical load. Nothing is executed; the prototype records the decision." className="mx-auto max-w-lg" />

**Approve and Reject is the wrong pair.** The choices are Approve, Defer with a trigger, Approve with changes, and Reject. An override needs a reason: expert disagreement with the model is the knowledge the industry is losing.

**The learning layer is not the product.** Real operators know what a PDU is, so domain teaching is a separate layer, off by default.

<CaseStudySection id="unguided" title="Built for a visitor on their own" eyebrow="The unguided visitor">

In a portfolio, nobody walks the visitor through it. So a seven-step tour runs on the first visit.

- **Every step asks for a real action**, not a description of one.
- **Steps complete on state, not on clicks.** Exploring ahead is never punished.
- **"Do it for me" covers every task** except the consequential approval.
- **Phones get their own page** instead of a squeezed console.

<CaseStudySection id="technical" title="Where the engineering went" eyebrow="Building the prototype">

**Impact is computed, not written.** The preview removes the selected asset and reruns reachability from the utility source. The topology is simplified and does not model generators.

**Accessibility is part of the work.** Nodes are keyboard selectable, dialogs trap focus, and every colored state is named in text.

**How it was built.** I built it with an AI pair in React 19, TypeScript, Vite, and React Flow. I wrote the plans and acceptance criteria, checked every result in a real browser, and made every product and design call. The AI wrote most of the code.

</CaseStudySection>

<CaseStudySection id="limits" title="The next test needs a real operator" eyebrow="Limits and next steps">

Reasoning and confidence figures are labeled illustrative. Decisions persist in the browser only. I do not claim to know data center operations, only enough to build something an expert can correct.

Next:

1. Test it with one real operator. Six fields felt right to me, but nobody who runs a facility has confirmed that.
2. An authoring flow. How does a tired operator at 18:55 write one honest sentence about why?
3. A timeline that shows model confidence and human decisions together.
