<CaseStudySection id="context" title="Help should leave the student able to continue" eyebrow="Context and ownership">

Flexi is CK-12's AI student tutor. As Lead UX Designer, I worked with Product, Engineering, Data Science, curriculum, and research partners. I turned the tutor's capabilities into interactions students could use.

My goal: help students past confusion and keep the learning effort with them. CK-12's platform reach does not measure this feature's impact.

</CaseStudySection>

<CaseStudySection id="tension" title="An answer can satisfy the request and miss the learning goal" eyebrow="The tension">

Refusing help can drive a student away. A bare answer turns the tutor into a shortcut. The middle path: help now, then a clear way to clarify, practice, or go deeper.

</CaseStudySection>

<CaseStudySection id="decision-scaffolding" title="Make the next learning move easy to ask for" eyebrow="Decision 01">

A blank prompt assumes a student knows how to ask for better help. The interface shows follow-up actions instead: simplify, add detail, use an analogy, translate, rephrase, or try a challenge.

The tradeoff: a menu asks a confused student for one more decision. So each choice must be clear.

</CaseStudySection>

<CaseStudySection id="decision-uncertainty" title="Give the student a way to inspect and recover" eyebrow="Decision 02">

The interface labels AI-generated content apart from CK-12 Library explanations. Students can ask for a different one. A source label is not a confidence estimate, but it helps the student orient.

<CaseStudySection id="research" title="Later classroom research sharpens the question" eyebrow="Published evidence">

I did not author this research. A [Spring 2025 Flexi study](https://info.ck12.org/impact-team-studies/flexi-ck-12s-ai-student-tutor-spring-2025-study) by Leanlab Education, an external nonprofit, followed ten middle- and high-school teachers in five states over six weeks. Teachers reported more student engagement, confidence, and curiosity. They also named barriers: students struggled to write prompts, and some teachers worried about over-reliance. Leanlab calls these early indicators, not conclusive.

In a [CK-12 comparison](https://info.ck12.org/impact-team-studies/teacher-comparative-test-overview), nine of ten teachers preferred Flexi to Khanmigo and Quizlet's Q-Chat. All ten named its visual, interactive responses as an advantage.

The follow-up actions address that prompting barrier.

</CaseStudySection>

<CaseStudySection id="system" title="The follow-up is part of the answer" eyebrow="Interaction model">

An explanation, its source, and the next learning action belong together. Split them, and recovery from a weak answer takes more work.

</CaseStudySection>

<CaseStudySection id="outcomes" title="Activity is not the same as learning" eyebrow="Evaluation">

CK-12's [analysis of student dialogue](https://info.ck12.org/impact-team-studies/the-dialogue-that-drives-learning-with-ai-opportunities-for-growth-and-intervention) sampled 5,000 students and studied 15 cases closely. Students who grew asked follow-ups. Students who did not tended to copy and paste.

In Adaptive Practice, Flexi offers "Give Me a Hint" and "Clarify This" and does not reveal the answer. There, direct answer-seeking questions fell from 72% of students' first questions to 52% by the eighth. Learning-oriented questions rose from 11% to 26%.

The analysis is descriptive. It does not show that my design caused a learning gain. It does support designing for conversation quality over length.

</CaseStudySection>

<CaseStudySection id="learnings" title="Test whether the understanding carries forward" eyebrow="Next question">

I would test follow-up choices by asking students to explain or apply what they learned. The question is whether they can proceed on their own.
