<CaseStudySection id="context" title="A prediction still leaves a teacher with a decision" eyebrow="Context">

Before an assignment, a teacher needs to know who may need support. After it, what to do next. One score cannot answer both.

In CK-12's [focus groups](https://info.ck12.org/impact-team-studies/focus-group-2), most of the eight teachers asked for class trends on upcoming content. Six cited a lack of transparency in AI assessment as a reason for distrust.

My goal: make the model's signals useful without making them look more certain than they were.

</CaseStudySection>

<CaseStudySection id="role" title="What I decided" eyebrow="My role">

As Lead UX Designer, I owned the experience architecture, interaction model, prototypes, and visual language for both tools. I started on Insights in 2020 and on Foresights in 2021. Both were live for teachers by May 2023, when CK-12 published its [introduction video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4Jgzjr1mleM).

I worked with four groups, and each owned a different question. Educators told us what the data meant in a classroom. Product set scope and priorities. Data Science decided what the model could responsibly claim. Engineering owned feasibility and delivery. I turned classroom needs and model limits into designs we could test, then worked with Engineering through delivery.

I framed the work around three questions a teacher moves through: what might happen, what happened, and what to do next. Three rules decided close calls: lead with the takeaway and show model detail on demand; show why a signal exists and never present it as fact; suggest a next step and let the teacher decide.

I did not run the research. CK-12's research team ran the focus groups and the two teacher studies below.

</CaseStudySection>

<CaseStudySection id="decision-prediction" title="Separate prediction from diagnosis" eyebrow="Decision 01">

One performance score is easy to scan but blurs two jobs: planning and investigation. I kept Foresights and Insights apart so a prediction does not pose as an explanation.

<CaseStudySection id="decision-uncertainty" title="Show a range, then help someone interpret it" eyebrow="Decision 02">

A single score was simple but misleading. A raw probability was honest but hard to read between classes. I chose the skill levels teachers already use, from Beginning to Mastery, and showed each student as a range on that scale (see the demo-class chart at the top). But teachers still need to know what a range lets them conclude.

<CaseStudySection id="decision-investigation" title="Keep skill and engagement separate" eyebrow="Decision 03">

Low performance can come with different patterns of effort, and one score hides that. A student who works hard but is stuck needs a different move than one who understands the concept with little activity. Insights separates skill from engagement, so a teacher can spot a class pattern, then check one student before acting. Selecting a student opens the evidence behind the dot and ends in a suggested next step, which the teacher accepts or ignores.

<CaseStudySection id="system" title="The interface depends on the evidence beneath it" eyebrow="System consequence">

Evidence is easier to act on when it maps to one concept. Assignments that span several concepts blur the result. Chart styling cannot fix that. CK-12 later moved to one concept per assignment and named better Foresights and Insights as a benefit. That platform decision was not mine alone.

<CaseStudySection id="evaluation" title="Teachers saw value. Interpretation still needed work." eyebrow="Evaluation">

Each CK-12 study used ten teachers and a demo class. They measured perceived usefulness and comprehension, not learning gains or time saved.

Graph difficulty offsets the usefulness ratings. A valuable concept can still demand too much interpretation.

</CaseStudySection>

<CaseStudySection id="learnings" title="Answer the finding with an action, not a better chart" eyebrow="What shipped next">

Half the teachers struggled with the main skill chart. We did not answer that with a better chart. We used the findings to build Recommendations, and it shipped to teachers: one action button for each insight, with a next step the system recommends. For example: "8 of your students struggled on this part. We recommend assigning this remedial content."

The teacher can act in one click. The skill chart becomes supporting detail, not the thing a teacher has to decode first.

For the student side, see [Flexi: helping students get unstuck](/work/flexi).
